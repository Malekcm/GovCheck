# Research provenance and evidence quality

## Source priority
1. Live official RFP/RFQ, all amendments, Q&A, SOW/PWS, attachments, eBuy if authorized; latest controls.
2. Official agency / DHS APFS / acquisition portal and historical version/change log.
3. Official GSA eLibrary/eBuy program documents and contractor domain details; SAM entity and solicitations.
4. USAspending, FPDS-derived federal award data, agency procurement data and GAO decisions.
5. Historical PWS, predecessor RFQ/award and federal procurement statutes/regulations; historical explicitly labeled.
6. Reputable secondary databases (HigherGov, GovTribe, etc.) for discovery; cross-verify material claims.
7. Internally approved capability statements, CPARS, past-performance writeups and records with owner approval.
8. User provided internal assertions and analytical interpretation, with INTERNAL_UNVALIDATED/INFERRED label.

For any claim record: unique ID, question ID, assertion, status, source title/URL or file section, publisher, as-of/retrieved time, confidence rationale, responsible reviewer, if applicable last-verification date and recommended follow-up.

## Current solicitation trace
At a minimum query: exact forecast ID; current opportunity number; contract number; exact and shorter title; acronym / successor office name; incumbent; agency and NAICS; relevant PSC. Save attempted source, query phrase, date, hit/negative/unavailable and caveat. An absence from SAM search indexes does not establish absence from gated GSA eBuy. Link the directly confirmed notice only, not a guessed SAM deep link.

## Tricky financial and business claims
Clearly label each: forecast estimate or band, ceiling, obligations, paid outlays, funded task order value, options included, and annualized spend estimate. Never treat an IDIQ ceiling as guaranteed demand. When aggregators disagree, preserve conflict and prefer primary award records. Avoid implying the incumbent cannot bid merely because its visible entity is an unrestricted contractor; affiliated JV and set-aside rules require verification.

## Strength and gap evidence
Four independent levels: (1) user/company claim, (2) named internal program, (3) accepted deliverable or approved quantitative result, (4) cited external/contract performance validation. 'STRONG' may describe a company-asserted competence, but always call out proof needed. STRONG is not synonymous with proven proposal relevance. Map deliverable to PWS sentence and actual company role, NOT name similarity.

## No false authority
Never describe AI as having accessed private eBuy without connected credentials or a provided screenshot. Never imply legal size determination, confirmed awarded domain eligibility for this task order, final due date, clearance availability, or binding bid/no-bid approval without supporting evidence.
